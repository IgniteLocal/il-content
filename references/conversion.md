# Conversion — Turning Readers Into Calls

Ranking and citations get the right person on the page. Conversion is what makes the phone ring. The old prompts had "add a CTA" and an above-the-fold instinct; this file makes conversion a discipline. Much of this borrows offer-and-value thinking (Hormozi-style) that lived in Justin's personal library but never made it into client content.

## 1. The above-the-fold formula

The top third of every transactional page (home, service, location) must answer five questions fast, because most readers decide whether to stay in seconds:

1. **What do you do?** (the service, in plain words)
2. **Who is it for / what problem does it solve?**
3. **Where do you do it?** (service area)
4. **Why you?** (one differentiator or proof point, not five)
5. **What do I do next?** (a visible CTA)

Structure it as: a keyphrase-led H1 that names the problem/service, a one-line supporting subhead with the differentiator, and a primary CTA. Everything else can come below the fold.

## 2. Value, made concrete

Conversion rises when the value is specific and the risk feels low. Translate features into outcomes the customer feels:
- Feature → outcome: "24/7 dispatch" → "someone answers when your basement is flooding at 2am."
- **Speed:** how fast they get relief ("same-day estimates," "we can be there today").
- **Certainty:** what's guaranteed, warrantied, or fixed-price.
- **Ease:** how little effort it takes them ("free on-site estimate, no obligation").

Name the outcome the reader actually wants (dry basement, safe roof, car that looks new, feeling better), not the process abstraction.

## 3. CTA discipline

- **One primary action per page**, repeated at natural decision points (after the hero, after proof, at the end). Don't scatter competing CTAs.
- **Match the client's real intake path.** Call, book online, request a free estimate, get a quote, schedule a consult. Use the verb that fits how *this* business takes business.
- **Give both a call and a form option** where possible. Some people call; some won't pick up the phone.
- **Make CTAs specific.** "Get your free roof inspection" beats "Contact us." "Book your fertility consult" beats "Learn more."
- **Rotate CTA wording** across a package so pages don't feel copy-pasted, but keep the action consistent per client.

## 4. Objection handling and risk reversal

Every prospect has silent objections. Answer them in the copy:
- **Price uncertainty** → free/no-obligation estimates, transparent pricing ranges, financing if offered.
- **Trust / "will they rip me off"** → license/insurance, warranties, reviews, "we explain before we do the work."
- **Risk of choosing wrong** → guarantees, satisfaction promises, warranty length. **Risk reversal is the highest-leverage conversion move** — if the client offers a guarantee, feature it prominently.
- **Timing** → availability, response time, emergency service.

Only use guarantees and terms the client actually offers. Never invent a guarantee. If a page would clearly benefit from one and you don't know their policy, add `⚑ VERIFY:` suggesting they consider it.

## 5. Social proof placement

- Put a proof point **near the first CTA** (a rating, a review count, a trust badge) so the reader acts with confidence.
- Use **specific proof** over vague ("4.9 stars across 300+ reviews" beats "highly rated"), but only if real.
- Weave **micro-proof** through the body: "since 2004," "over 5,000 roofs," named certifications.
- A **testimonial or short case detail** below the fold reinforces desire before the closing CTA.

## 6. Friction reduction

- Make the **phone number visible and clickable**; state hours and response expectations.
- Keep **forms short** (name, phone, service, zip is usually enough for a lead). Recommend trimming long forms if you see one.
- **Set expectations:** "We'll call you back within an hour during business hours."
- Name the **service area** clearly so out-of-area readers self-select out and in-area readers feel served.

## 7. Intent-appropriate conversion by page type

- **Service / location / home:** full conversion treatment. Lead them to act.
- **About:** convert on trust. Close with a soft CTA after establishing credibility.
- **FAQ:** answer helpfully first; end with a light "still have questions? call us" nudge. Don't hard-sell.
- **Blog:** teach genuinely. Earn the click with one relevant, low-pressure CTA (usually a link to the related service page plus a single end CTA). A blog that reads like a sales pitch loses the informational ranking and the reader's trust. This is the most common conversion-vs-intent mistake — respect the informational contract.
