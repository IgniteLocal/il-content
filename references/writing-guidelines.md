# Writing Guidelines — The Voice Layer

This is the always-on quality bar. It applies to every page, blog, FAQ, and refresh, in every workflow. Read it every session. It is the consolidated, de-duplicated successor to the old "General Writing Guideline," "New Page Writing," "Page SEO Refresh," and Nov-2025 rewrite docs — with the mutations and typos removed and the AI-search era baked in.

## 1. Role and mindset

Before writing for a client, **adopt the role of an experienced operator in that exact trade** — a master plumber, a board-certified naturopath, a collision-shop owner of 30 years. Write from inside the business's expertise, addressing a real local customer who has a problem right now.

Two voices to avoid at all costs:
- **The robot.** Even, hedged, list-happy, allergic to a strong sentence. Reads like it was generated.
- **The agency.** Buzzword-fluent, benefit-vague, in love with its own adjectives. Reads like a brochure written by someone who's never done the work.

The target voice sits between "knowledgeable" and "neighborly." Confident, plain, specific. If you'd be embarrassed to say a sentence out loud to a customer standing in their driveway, cut it.

## 2. Reading level and rhythm

- **Default to an 8th–9th grade reading level.** This is not dumbing down; it's respect for a busy reader on a phone. Short words beat long ones. One idea per sentence.
- Flex up **only** for genuinely technical or regulated verticals (law, medical, industrial, financial). Even then, explain jargon the first time and keep sentences short.
- **Vary sentence length** for rhythm. A short punch after two medium sentences keeps a reader moving. All-medium-length sentences are what AI text sounds like.
- Read it aloud in your head. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.

## 3. The AIDA arc

Structure every page and blog on **Attention → Interest → Desire → Action**:
- **Attention** — the H1 and opening line name the reader's problem or goal in their words.
- **Interest** — show you understand the situation; give the useful, specific information.
- **Desire** — make the outcome and the "why this business" concrete: proof, process, differentiators, guarantees.
- **Action** — one clear next step.

AIDA is the page-level skeleton. Inside each section, use answer-first (section 7).

## 4. Natural language: sound human, not AI

The single most valuable thing this skill protects is copy that doesn't read as machine-made. Concrete tactics:

- **Lead with substance, not throat-clearing.** Cut "In today's fast-paced world," "When it comes to," "Whether you're X or Y," and "As a homeowner, you know."
- **Prefer concrete nouns and verbs** over abstractions. "We replace burst pipes the same day" beats "We provide comprehensive solutions for your plumbing needs."
- **No hedging pile-ups.** "can help to potentially improve" → "improves."
- **Don't over-list.** AI reflexively turns everything into three-item lists with parallel structure. Use prose where prose is clearer; use a list only when the reader genuinely benefits from scanning discrete items.
- **Kill the summary sentence that restates the paragraph.** AI loves to end a paragraph by re-summarizing it. Delete those.
- **No em dashes.** They are a strong AI tell for our purposes. Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead. (This is a house rule — honor it even though em dashes are grammatically fine.)
- **Contractions are good.** "You'll," "we're," "don't" read human. Corporate copy avoids them; we don't.
- **Specificity is the antidote.** Real detail (a neighborhood name, a real service step, a real warranty length) is the fastest way to stop sounding generated.

## 5. The banned list

Never use these. They are empty intensifiers, agency clichés, and AI tells. This is the merged list from every prior version.

**AI-tell adjectives / intensifiers:**
top-notch, industry-leading, cutting-edge, seamless, seamless experience, unparalleled, unparalleled quality, state-of-the-art, next-level, revolutionary, world-class, game-changing, game-changer, second to none, best-in-class, premier, unrivaled, superior (as a bare claim), elevate, elevate your, unlock, unleash, robust, bespoke, curated, meticulous, meticulously, nestled, in the heart of, boasts, testament to, stands as a testament.

**SEO / marketing filler phrases:**
look no further, your go-to, go-to solution, tried and true, must-have, highly sought-after, crafted with care, designed to perfection, exceed expectations, exceeding expectations, tailored to your needs, one-stop shop, when it comes to, rest assured, peace of mind (as filler), we've got you covered, take it to the next level, at the end of the day, in today's fast-paced world.

**Structural / punctuation bans:**
- Em dashes (—). Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- Opening a piece with "In today's..." / "In the world of..." / "Whether you're..."
- Ending a section by restating what it just said.

If a banned phrase is genuinely the only way to say something (rare), rewrite the sentence so it isn't needed. There is always a plainer, truer way.

## 6. Keyphrase discipline without stuffing

The primary keyphrase must appear in:
- the **H1** (as naturally as possible),
- the **first ~100 words**,
- the **meta title** (near the front),
- the **URL slug**,
- and **naturally** through the body a few times.

Rules:
- **Readability wins every time.** If placing the keyphrase makes a sentence clunky, reword the sentence, don't force the phrase.
- **Use variants and secondary keyphrases** rather than repeating the exact primary phrase over and over. Search engines and AI models understand synonyms and entities; humans notice repetition.
- **Never** write a sentence whose only purpose is to hold a keyphrase.
- Say the keyphrase out loud in context. If it sounds like SEO, it is. Fix it.

## 7. Formatting for skimmers and answer engines

Structure serves two readers: the human scanning on a phone, and the AI trying to lift a clean answer. The same tactics serve both.

- **Descriptive, question-style H2s** that mirror how people actually search ("How much does collision repair cost in Bellevue?"), not vague labels ("Our Services").
- **Answer-first sections.** Open each section with a direct, self-contained answer in 1–2 sentences, then explain. An AI answer engine can quote the opener; a skimmer gets the point immediately.
- **Short paragraphs**, 1–3 sentences.
- **Lists and tables** for anything genuinely enumerable (steps, options, specs, pricing tiers) — these are highly extractable by AI.
- **Bold** only true emphasis, a few words at a time. Bold everywhere = emphasis nowhere.
- **One H1 per page.** Logical H2 → H3 nesting.

## 8. Truthfulness and E-E-A-T

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trust — this is what earns both Google rankings and AI citations, and it's what converts.

- **Use only real signals.** Years in business, licenses and certifications (with numbers where public), named service areas, warranty/guarantee terms, review counts and ratings, real process steps, real team credentials, memberships, insurance.
- **If you don't have a signal, don't fabricate it.** Request it, or write the line without it and add a `⚑ VERIFY:` note listing what would strengthen the page.
- **Attribute concrete claims.** "Licensed and insured (CSLB #123456)" beats "fully licensed." "Rated 4.9 across 300+ Google reviews" beats "highly rated" — but only if it's true.
- **First person for the business.** "We've repaired storm damage across North Alabama since 2004." This reads human and signals real experience.

## 9. Before / after rewrites

**Example 1 — service page opener**
- Before: *In today's fast-paced world, when it comes to your plumbing needs, look no further than our top-notch, industry-leading team of experts who are second to none.*
- After: *A burst pipe won't wait for business hours, and neither do we. We're licensed plumbers serving Bellingham, and we answer the phone 24/7 for emergencies.*

**Example 2 — over-listed AI paragraph**
- Before: *Our services include: professional installation, reliable maintenance, and expert repair. We are committed to quality, dedicated to service, and focused on customer satisfaction.*
- After: *We install, maintain, and repair residential HVAC systems. Most repair calls are handled the same day, and every install comes with a 10-year parts warranty.*

**Example 3 — answer-first for AI + skimmers**
- Before (buried): *There are many factors that can influence the cost of tree removal, and every situation is unique, so it's difficult to say exactly what you might pay without more information about your specific circumstances.*
- After (answer-first): *Most tree removals in Lake Stevens run $400 to $1,800. The price depends on the tree's height, its proximity to your house or power lines, and whether you want the stump ground out. Here's how each factor moves the number.*

**Example 4 — killing the em dash and the summary sentence**
- Before: *Our team is highly experienced — with decades of combined expertise — and we always go the extra mile to ensure your satisfaction, because at the end of the day, your satisfaction is what matters most to us.*
- After: *Our lead technicians each have 15+ years on the job. If something isn't right after we leave, we come back and fix it at no charge.*
