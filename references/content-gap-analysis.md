# Content Gap Analysis + Blog Topic Ideation

Two related deliverables that plan what to write: the **content gap analysis** (which pages the site is missing at launch or now) and the **blog topic list** (15–20 ongoing topic ideas for client review). Both are grounded in the same content-cluster strategy.

## Table of contents
1. The content-cluster model
2. Content gap analysis (pages)
3. Blog topic list (15–20 ideas)

---

## 1. The content-cluster model

Organize a client's content as **clusters**: a **pillar page** (a core service) supported by **blog posts** that answer the common questions customers ask about that service. The pillar sells; the supporting posts teach and link up to it.

Why it matters for all four goals:
- **SEO:** clusters build topical authority, so the whole group ranks better than isolated pages.
- **GEO/AEO:** depth across a topic makes the business a credible source AI engines cite, and question-based posts are exactly what answer engines pull from.
- **Client reflection:** thorough, helpful coverage signals genuine expertise.
- **Conversion:** supporting posts capture early-stage searchers and route them to the pillar's CTA.

Every gap-analysis recommendation and every blog topic should map to a pillar (service) page.

## 2. Content gap analysis (pages)

Compare three things: **what the client offers** (from intake), **what competitors rank for** (top 3 local competitors), and **what the site currently has** (or, for a new build, a strong template for the vertical).

Surface:
- **Missing service pages** — services the client offers with no dedicated page. Each is a ranking + conversion opportunity being left on the table.
- **Missing supporting pages** — location pages for real service areas, a dedicated FAQ page, financing, warranty/guarantee, "areas we serve."
- **Consolidation issues** — one page trying to rank for several distinct services (split it), or thin near-duplicate pages (combine them).
- **Intent gaps** — competitors capturing informational intent the client ignores (fuels the blog list).

**Output format:**
```
CONTENT GAP ANALYSIS — [Client]

Current taxonomy: [list, or "net-new build"]

Recommended pages to add at launch:
1. [Page] — [why: service offered, competitor ranking, search demand] — Priority: High/Med
2. ...

Consolidate / split:
- [recommendation + why]

Recommended final taxonomy:
Home | About | [services...] | [location pages] | FAQ | Blog | Contact
```

Confirm the recommended taxonomy with the account owner before building the Content Blueprint against it.

## 3. Blog topic list (15–20 ideas)

The client-facing list that seeds ongoing monthly blog production. Each topic is a supporting post mapped to a pillar service page. Deliver 15–20 so the client has a quarter-plus of runway to review and approve.

**Selection rules:**
- **Informational, not transactional.** These teach; the service pages sell. Favor questions real customers ask (cost, how-to-choose, how-it-works, comparisons, problems/symptoms, maintenance, "is X worth it").
- **Educational/consultative, not DIY-that-cannibalizes-the-service.** "How to know if your roof needs replacing" is good; "How to replace your own roof" trains the customer to not hire them. Position the business as the expert.
- **Map each to a pillar** (service) page so the cluster builds.
- **Localize where it helps** (city-specific angles rank and convert locally).
- **AEO-friendly:** phrase titles as the questions people ask, so they're quotable by AI and clickworthy in search. No em dashes in titles.
- **No cannibalization:** distinct primary keyphrases across the list and against existing content.

**Output format (per topic):**
```
BLOG TOPIC LIST — [Client]  (for client review)

Cluster: [Pillar / Service page]
1. Title: "[Question-style, clickworthy, AI-quotable]"
   Primary Keyphrase: [phrase]
   Supporting Keywords: [2–4]
   Intent: [what the searcher wants]
   Why it supports the pillar: [1 line]
   Suggested Internal Links: [→ pillar page, → related]

2. ...

Cluster: [next Pillar / Service page]
...
```

Group by cluster so the client sees the strategy, not just a list. Note at the top that this is for their review and approval, and that approved topics feed monthly blog production (Workflow 2).
